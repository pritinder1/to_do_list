The four pillars

Four areas. One alliance.

AGMA focuses on four key areas. Each erodes something different: margin, safety, the authorised channel, or the trust a brand spent decades earning. Most members are dealing with all four at once.

Gray Marketing

Genuine product sold outside the channel it was authorized for. Region locks broken, discounts arbitraged, distributor agreements quietly ignored, and no warranty path when the customer needs one.

Pillar
Preventing unauthorised distribution

Counterfeiting

Fake components and whole units built to pass a glance and fail under load. In networking, medical and industrial equipment, a counterfeit part is not a pricing problem. It is a safety incident waiting for a date.

Pillar
Stopping fake goods

Service & Warranty Abuse

Support contracts claimed on units that were never entitled to them. Coverage resold, entitlements cloned, replacement parts harvested. The cost lands in service operations long before anyone calls it fraud.

Pillar
Addressing fraudulent claims

Digital IP Protection

Firmware, licence keys and software images lifted, re-signed and redistributed. As product value moves from the box to the code inside it, protecting digital IP has become the whole ballgame.

Pillar
Safeguarding IP in the digital space

How the alliance works

Competitors in the market. Allies on this.

AGMA gives brand protection teams a room they cannot build inside their own company: one where the people facing the same counterfeiters compare notes under a shared confidentiality framework.

STEP 01

Convene the practitioners

Brand protection, channel, legal and supply chain leads meet in a standing open forum. What one member sees this quarter, the rest hear about before it reaches their own channel.

STEP 02

Benchmark what actually works

Benchmark studies turn scattered internal experience into comparable data, so a member can see how their detection rates, vetting process and enforcement spend measure against the field.

STEP 03

Publish the guidance

Findings become white papers and industry guidelines any manufacturer can act on, covering reseller vetting, authentication practice, entitlement controls and digital IP defence.

STEP 04

Put it in front of enforcement

The alliance briefs customs, law enforcement and policymakers, and equips them directly, so the people inspecting freight know what a counterfeit network unit looks like when it reaches the dock.

Enforcement partnership

From the working group to the port of entry.

Through U.S. Customs and Border Protection’s Donations Acceptance Program, AGMA entered a donation agreement to deploy the One Device handheld to CBP officers, putting IP inspection capability in the hands of the people who see the freight first.

It is the clearest expression of what the alliance is for. Member intelligence is worth little if it stops at the member’s own firewall.
